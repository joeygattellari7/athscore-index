AthScore Index

Import athlete seasons from any sport, score them on one scale, and rank them. Every score is built from competition rank, recency and sample size, and you can change each setting and watch the ranking move.

1. Import data

    About the Premier League index

    Every player who has played a minute in the Premier League from 1992-93 to the current season (about 5,300 players). 1992-93 to 2015-16 come from the open epldata database (appearances, starts, goals, assists, cards, clean sheets). 2016-17 onwards come from the Fantasy Premier League data (adds expected goals, expected assists, saves and bonus points). Each season is scored as a percentile against other players in the same position that season. The two eras use different inputs, so scores are close but not exactly like for like. Top flight before 1992 and appearances outside the Premier League are not included.

    About the soccer index (version 1)

    61 men’s players, built from the France Football Ballon d’Or final top 30 for 2024, 2025 and 2026 (the 2026 list covers August 2025 to July 2026). Each list position becomes a performance score with the same rule as the pilot: 100 × (1 − ln(rank) ÷ ln(200)). Season labels are the award year.

    Limits. This is a voted award, not match statistics, and it only sees players in the top 30 each year, so a strong player outside it (or one who dropped out) has no row, not a low score. It does not yet include the 2026-27 season now under way, and it has no players outside the men’s game. Birth years come from general knowledge, not the sources. Tied places in the sources (2024 and 2026, rank 29) are scored as the same rank. Retired or injured players such as Kroos and Carvajal appear only if you tick include inactive. Next step: replace these scores with per-game statistics from a licensed or open data feed.

    About the real pilot data

    93 real athletes across tennis (men and women), Formula 1, football (men and women), basketball, golf, rugby league and AFL, from the 2024, 2025 and 2026 seasons. Each season is one published ranking: ATP and WTA year-end rank, F1 championship position, Ballon d'Or rank, NBA MVP voting rank, world golf ranking, Dally M Medal count and Brownlow Medal count.

    The perf score is worked out from rank with one rule for every sport: 100 × (1 − ln(rank) ÷ ln(200)). Rank 1 scores 100, rank 5 about 70, rank 30 about 36. Athletes missing from a published list have no row for that season, so they are not penalised for it.

    Treat this as a plumbing test, not a verdict. It compares very different ranking lists, every row is tier 1, and games is set to a nominal 40 for full seasons (17 for the part-played 2026 F1 season). Season 2026 covers rankings published up to 25 October 2026, so athletes from lists not yet out for 2026 show only earlier seasons. Birth years come from general knowledge, not from the sources, and a few rankings (such as 2024 positions for Sinner and Alcaraz) were filled from memory.

    Column guide

    athlete sport season tier games are required, plus either perf or stat.

    perf is a 0 to 100 score against peers in the same sport and position (a percentile works well). If perf is blank, the tool converts stat (any number where higher is better) into a percentile within the same sport, position, competition and season. Fewer than about 10 peers in a group gives rough percentiles.

    tier is competition rank from 1 (top in the world) to 6 (community). games is matches or appearances, which sets how much to trust the season. position, competition and birth_year are optional. Use one season convention throughout, such as the year the season ends.

    2. Scoring settings

    Honours: AthScore = performance score × (100 − cap) ÷ 100, plus title and award points up to the cap. A title counts when the player made 10+ appearances for the champions. Awards are Golden Boot, Player of the Season and Golden Glove.

    Form 60%Career 40%

    A perf score is multiplied by its tier's number, so 90 at tier 3 (0.70) counts as 63 on the shared scale.

    Tier guide: 1 top competition in the world for the sport. 2 strong second tier. 3 national top flight in a smaller market. 4 semi-professional. 5 state or regional elite. 6 community.
